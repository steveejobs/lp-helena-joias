# Discovery and Strategy Reference

## Evidence Ledger

Build a discovery map before making recommendations.

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Business classification | Site type and rationale |
| Acquisition model | Channels, geography, buyer type, conversion model |
| Brand and offer | Name, proposition, products, services, categories |
| Audience and market | Confirmed audience, language, country/region, regulation |
| Local presence | Real units, areas served, NAP, hours, routes, proof |
| Conversion | Primary action, secondary actions, existing paths |
| Maturity | Technical, content, trust, measurement, conversion maturity |
| Gaps and ambiguity | Missing facts and the decision they block |

Tag every line as `confirmed_by_project`, `inferred_high_confidence`, `inferred_low_confidence`, `needs_confirmation`, or `external_research`. Do not convert an inference into copy, schema, or a production claim.

## Classification Signals

- **Local business:** address, hours, maps, real service area, call/route/visit paths.
- **E-commerce/catalog:** category/product routes, price, stock, cart, checkout, filters, fulfillment.
- **SaaS/web app:** authentication, app/dashboard routes, plans, trials, integrations, documentation.
- **Service business:** consultation, quote, booking, case studies, service routes, lead forms.
- **Publisher/portal:** taxonomy, author bylines, editorial updates, archives, topic hubs.
- **Hybrid:** record both public acquisition and private/product surfaces separately.

## Product, Service, and Location Matrix

For every real product or service, record name, category, description, audience, problem solved, search intent, funnel stage, locality, differentiator, proof, CTA, existing URL, justified missing URL, supporting content, applicable schema, and cannibalization risk.

For each locality, record city/state/country/neighborhood only when commercially relevant, real presence/coverage, service availability, unique local facts, proof, NAP, hours, route, local questions, and local CTA. A locality page requires substantial unique value such as a real unit, logistics, team, case, testimonial, condition, local regulation, availability, or route.

## Research Protocol

Research externally only after the project is understood. Prefer first-party documentation, official registries, primary data, credible market research, SERP observations, maps/listings, and verifiable reviews. Record source URL and verification date. Separate project facts, external facts, hypotheses, and recommendations.

Use competitor research to find language, problems, objections, criteria, formats, entities, and gaps. Do not copy their pages or assume their claims apply to the project.

## Information Architecture and Content

Audit intent and internal relationships before creating a URL. A page exists only if it has a clear audience, intent, information gain, commercial relationship, parent/hub, inbound and outbound links, CTA, maintenance owner, and no equivalent existing page.

Possible building blocks include products, services, solutions by segment/problem, real locations, guides, comparisons, questions, cases, about/contact, and the primary conversion path. Select only the blocks warranted by the business.

Treat content as a growth lever only when it can educate, answer, reduce risk, establish expertise, support a decision, or provide original evidence. Use a hub-and-spoke cluster only when the pillar and supporting content have distinct intents and can be maintained.

## GEO/AEO and Entity Clarity

Use summary-first writing for important pages when it benefits readers: direct answer, explanation, proof/example, limitation/condition, next action. Make sections meaningful when extracted by search or answer systems. Show the organization, offer, location, author/responsible party, sources, and update context clearly where factual evidence exists.

GEO/AEO extends, rather than replaces, SEO, authority, usability, and demand. Cite potential is not guaranteed by schema, backlinks, any platform, or a formatting pattern.

## UX, Accessibility, Performance, and Measurement

Match CTAs to intent stage. Evaluate value clarity, proof, friction, form quality, responsive behavior, keyboard navigation, focus, accessible names, landmarks, alt text, labels, errors, and touch targets. Validate browser behavior with real geometry when tools are available.

Prioritize critical content, interaction, and stability. Connect performance findings to the affected resource and user outcome. Define privacy-aware events around meaningful action, not surveillance: CTA click, form state, phone, WhatsApp, map, booking, purchase, demo, activation, relevant scroll, filter/search, and referral conversion.
